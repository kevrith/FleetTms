# Driver monitoring notice (DRAFT draft-2)

Shown to drivers and turnboys at first sign-in, and again whenever it changes. They must accept it before using the app. Owners can reuse the wording in employment contracts.

**What is recorded.** Your phone's location (position, speed and direction) while a trip is active, odometer photos, fuel and expense entries, delivery photos and signatures, and basic information about your phone that helps confirm records are genuine (for example whether a fake-location app is in use).

**Why.** To run trips, show the office where the lorry is, check distance against the odometer, notice when a phone goes quiet during a trip, prevent fuel theft and false claims, keep you safe, and settle disputes with clients.

**When.** Location is recorded only while a trip is running. It starts when you start the trip and stops when you end it. The system refuses any location from before a trip started or after it ended. It does not run outside work, and it records nothing else on your phone (no contacts, messages or personal photos). While it runs, the phone shows a notification so you can see tracking is on.

**Who sees it.** Your employer, and the supervisors and managers they choose. A client you are delivering for can follow only that delivery's progress and expected arrival on a link the office sends; the link shows no driver details and stops working when the delivery is made. If your employer's vehicle is leased from another owner, that owner sees location and trip details only if your employer's lease agreement allows it.

**How long.** Location points are kept for 12 months and then deleted; only each trip's totals (such as distance) are kept.

**Your rights.** You can ask your employer to show you, correct or delete your data, and you can complain to the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner.

**Scores and alerts.** Driver scores and alerts help coaching. A person must review them before any disciplinary decision.
